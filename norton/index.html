Climate control is your most expensive bill. It never clocks out.

Galleries hold about 70 degrees and 50 percent humidity around the clock: air cooled to wring the water out of it, then warmed back to temperature, every hour of the year. That is where the most waste sits, and one way we cut the peak is by pulling that lever, without moving a setpoint.

How we cut the peak, retrofit what you already have, and replace only what is done.

Retrofit what you have. Replace only what is done.

Chillers. Air handlers. Rooftop units. A restaurant, an auditorium, a great hall. A new system still has waste in it. An old one still has life in it. We match the fix to the machine, in the 1941 building and the 2019 building alike: more output, less service, and the gold standard only when a unit is finished.

  • For any refrigerant circuit · either wing, any age

    The coil treatment

    Compressor oil bakes onto the inside of the coil circuit. A wash never reaches it. One refrigerant-side treatment lifts it off, for the life of the unit, including one that is new.

    Recovers up to 36% of lost performance1, 12

  • For simple on/off compressors · rooftop and split systems

    The HVAC optimizer

    Learns how the space actually runs and shuts the compressor off the moment it stops doing useful work, while the fan keeps delivering the stored conditioning. Nothing changes in the galleries.

    15–30% less HVAC energy1

  • For inverter-type systems

    The solar coil

    Not a solar panel: a solar thermal retrofit that captures the sun's heat and puts it to work for the compressor. Sits with the equipment, out of sight. Nothing shows on the building.

    30–50% more efficient system1

  • For the panels that feed air handlers, pumps, and the restaurant

    Power conditioning

    Air handlers, restaurant equipment, and pumps do not draw power smoothly. Distortion becomes heat. Heat retires a machine early, and it fattens the peak. We clean the power that reaches them.

    A quieter peak, longer life1, 12
